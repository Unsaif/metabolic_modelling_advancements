# Sprint 3 — GEM-Bench across organisms: draft models against RB-TnSeq fitness

**5 September 2026 · Status: complete for the four bacterial organisms below; the whole-body IEM run from Sprint 2 is re-running in the background (see §7). All numbers were recomputed from the saved matrices by an independent script (`scripts/verify_carbon_fitness_multi.py`, zero discrepancies).**

## 1. What was built

Sprint 1 reproduced one published benchmark (Bernstein et al. 2023, E. coli against Fitness Browser carbon-source fitness) from the authors' own files. This sprint turns that into a general instrument: any organism with Fitness Browser data and any model in the BiGG namespace, with every mapping step explicit and versioned.

The pieces, all in the `gembench` package of the repository: the Fitness Browser tables for nine organisms, downloaded from fit.genomics.lbl.gov on 5 September (`data/fitness_browser/`, with provenance); the Fitness Browser's own media recipes, extracted from the FEBA code repository (bitbucket.org/berkeleylab/feba, `metadata/media` and `metadata/mixes`) and mapped by hand to BiGG components (`data/reference/fitness_browser_media_bigg.tsv`, six base media); a curated table mapping 135 Fitness Browser carbon-source names, 95 of them to BiGG metabolite identifiers, with a confidence grade and a reason for every unmapped compound (`data/reference/fitness_browser_carbon_sources_bigg.tsv`); a gene-identifier map from EMBL GEM gene names (RefSeq protein accessions) to Fitness Browser locus tags, obtained by fetching the NCBI GenPept record of every protein in the four models (4,054 records through the browser pane, parsed to `data/genpept/`) — coverage is complete: 674 of 674 real genes for B. thetaiotaomicron, 1,291 of 1,291 for P. putida, 891 of 894 for S. oneidensis (three plasmid genes absent from the Browser), 1,195 of 1,195 for S. meliloti; a generic protocol (`gembench/protocols/carbon_fitness_generic.py`) that applies the medium of each experiment, opens the carbon source at −10 mmol/gDW/h, computes wild-type growth, deletes every gene with fitness data, and scores at two levels; and a minimal gap-filler against the CarveMe bacterial universe, written on HiGHS so that the added reactions are explicit in every benchmark card (`gembench/gapfill.py`).

Two levels of scoring are reported because they answer different questions. The condition level asks whether the wild-type model grows on a carbon source the organism demonstrably grew on (every Fitness Browser carbon-source experiment is a positive, because fitness cannot be measured without growth); its summary is a growth recall. The gene level follows Bernstein et al.: on the conditions where the wild-type model grows, every gene with fitness data is deleted and the predicted growth (below 1e-3 = no growth) is compared with experimental fitness (below −2 = important), pooled over conditions, with the Bernstein AUC-PR convention, the standard AUROC, MCC and balanced accuracy, and gene-resampling bootstrap intervals. Genes essential with every exchange open are dropped first, as in the original protocol.

## 4. What the benchmark found

**None of the four draft models grows on the medium its fitness data were collected in.** As shipped, with the actual experimental medium applied and glucose (lactate for S. oneidensis) supplied, all four models are infeasible for biomass, and the reasons are specific and different. S. meliloti lacks 1-deoxy-D-xylulose-5-phosphate synthase (DXPS), which blocks thiamine, isoprenoids and therefore menaquinone and undecaprenyl-linked peptidoglycan at once; the organism has dxs, so this is a missed annotation. B. thetaiotaomicron, under anaerobic conditions, has no ferric-iron uptake for the biomass ferric-iron requirement, cannot run the oxygen-dependent steps the universe assigns to NAD, pyridoxal-phosphate and pyrimidine synthesis, and lacks ornithine carbamoyltransferase — five gaps, three of them the classic anaerobe problem of oxygen-dependent reactions chosen where NAD- or fumarate-dependent variants exist. S. oneidensis cannot oxidise L-lactate (its LldEFG dehydrogenase is absent), cannot make ornithine, and can take up zinc only through a zinc–citrate symporter, so without citrate in the medium it has no zinc; the organism's ZnuABC transporter is simply missing from the draft (the parsimonious fill satisfied this with a citrate-recycling antiporter, an equal-cost alternative to the transporter — the kind of tie a curator, not a solver, should break). P. putida, a strict aerobe, is blocked only on menaquinol, which its CarveMe biomass template demands although the organism uses ubiquinone; the two-reaction fix the gap-filler chose is an artefact of that template, and the better fix is to the biomass.

**Parsimonious gap-filling needs physical guard-rails.** The first fill for B. thetaiotaomicron satisfied all five anaerobic gaps with two reactions by running a reversible inositol oxygenase backwards to generate molecular oxygen inside the cell. A rule that no added reaction may run in an oxygen-releasing direction produced the five biologically sensible reactions above (`gembench.gapfill` has this rule on by default). This is a general point for the curation workstream: universal reaction databases carry reversibility annotations that a minimising solver will exploit, so every automated fill needs constraints that encode physics, and every fill should be printed, as the benchmark cards do.

**After a single-condition fill, condition-level recall is 56–67 percent, and the failures are of two clean kinds.** The models were gap-filled for growth on one reference carbon source only, so their behaviour on the other carbon sources is a genuine prediction. B. thetaiotaomicron grows on 14 of 25, P. putida on 28 of 43, S. oneidensis on 8 of 12, S. meliloti on 21 of 33. Where they fail, the condition tables (`results/carbon_fitness_multi/<org>/*/conditions.tsv`) split the failures into carbon sources with no exchange reaction at all — the draft has no transporter, for instance fructose, mannose, trehalose, N-acetylglucosamine and glucosamine in B. thetaiotaomicron, which uses all of them — and carbon sources with an exchange but no route to biomass, which are missing catabolic pathways: D-arabinose and maltose in B. thetaiotaomicron; butyrate, butanol, leucine, galacturonate and glucuronate in P. putida; propionate, N-acetylglucosamine, inosine and isoleucine in S. oneidensis; and, strikingly, succinate, arabinose and five amino acids in S. meliloti, an organism whose C4-dicarboxylate transport is textbook. Each is a concrete, testable curation target with a known answer.

**At the gene level the draft models are systematically too permissive, and the cause is identifiable.** False positives outnumber false negatives by 9 to 150 to one. The genes that are experimentally important on at least half of the growth conditions yet never predicted essential (32 in B. thetaiotaomicron, 23 in P. putida, 78 in S. oneidensis, 39 in S. meliloti) are dominated by amino-acid and nucleotide biosynthesis: anthranilate synthase, carbamoyl-phosphate synthase, dihydroxy-acid dehydratase, phosphoserine phosphatase, serine hydroxymethyltransferase, glutamate 5-kinase, the leucine pathway. Tracing the flux after knockout in the P. putida model shows how the model escapes: carbamoyl-phosphate synthase is bypassed by carbamate kinase written as reversible (CBMKr, an ADI-pathway enzyme that runs catabolically in vivo), and phosphoserine phosphatase by a promiscuous homoserine-kinase reaction (STPT1). Carbamoyl-phosphate synthase is "dispensable" in three of the four organisms for the same reason. These are universe-level errors — a reversibility flag or a promiscuous reaction in CarveMe's reaction database — so a single correction propagates to every CarveMe-derived model, which is the highest-leverage kind of curation there is and exactly the fix-then-verify loop the roadmap's WS1 describes.

## 5. First fix-at-source cycle (WS1, verifier-gated)

The carbamoyl-phosphate finding of §4 was taken through one full propose-then-verify cycle. Two universe-level corrections were written down with their biochemical justification before any re-scoring (`data/reference/universe_patches_v0.1.json`): carbamate kinase (CBMKr) restricted to its catabolic direction, because the enzyme phosphorylates ADP at the expense of carbamoyl phosphate in the arginine deiminase and allantoin pathways and is not a physiological route to carbamoyl phosphate in organisms with carAB; and the CBPS gene rule reduced to the carA–carB conjunction, because glutamine-dependent carbamoyl-phosphate synthetase is an obligate heterodimer and CarveMe's merged rules ('carA or carB or (carA and carB)', with the aspartate carbamoyltransferase pyrB as a further standalone alternative in S. oneidensis) let single-subunit knockouts through. The patches were applied to every model that carries the reactions and the whole benchmark re-run (`scripts/evaluate_patch.py`, results in `patch_v0.1_evaluation.tsv`).

Nothing switched in the other direction and no wild-type growth was lost. Of the 180 predictions the patches changed, 164 are supported by the fitness data and all 16 unsupported ones come from one organism: in B. thetaiotaomicron both carbamoyl-phosphate synthetase genes (BT0556, BT0557) have no fitness phenotype on the defined Varel–Bryant medium, which contains neither arginine nor pyrimidines. That is a genuine anomaly, not a scoring artefact — either the organism has a carbamoyl-phosphate source the annotation does not show, or pooled-assay cross-feeding rescues these particular auxotrophs — and it means the CBPS rule patch, biochemically sound as it is, is rejected for B. thetaiotaomicron until the anomaly is understood. iML1515 did not change because E. coli's model has a second bypass: oxamate carbamoyltransferase run in reverse, fed by de novo purine synthesis and degradation, a route that exists physiologically only during anaerobic allantoin degradation — a regulatory rather than a stoichiometric error, which the fitness data cannot fix and the loop has to leave for a different kind of constraint.

Two things this cycle establishes for WS1. The verifier caught something the justification missed (B. thetaiotaomicron), which is the point of gating. And the bypass analysis (`scripts/find_bypasses.py`, per-gene tables in `bypasses_<org>.tsv`) shows the shape of the remaining false positives: of the 172 persistently important genes the four draft models call dispensable, 75 are protected by gene rules that name a paralog as an isozyme — the same CarveMe merging that produced the carAB rule — and the rest by alternative reactions, most of them reversible steps in amino-acid metabolism (aspartate carbamoyltransferase, ornithine carbamoyltransferase, glycine hydroxymethyltransferase, alanine and aspartate transaminases).

**Second cycle: the 75 false-isozyme rules, adjudicated from annotation.** The 75 (organism, reaction) cases were listed with the RefSeq product name of every gene in the rule (`false_isozyme_candidates.tsv`) and adjudicated one by one under three written rules that never consult the fitness data: a gene whose product name denotes a different enzyme, a family or domain, or a non-enzymatic protein is removed from an OR rule; genes annotated as subunits of one complex are joined with AND; paralogs carrying the same product name are left alone. Fifty-two rules changed (`data/reference/gpr_patches_v0.2.json`, each with its reason) and twenty-two were left alone, including several the fitness data would have argued against (plasmid copies of dihydroxy-acid dehydratase and histidinol dehydrogenase in S. meliloti, cysK/cysM, purN/purT), because annotation cannot separate them. The removed genes are a catalogue of how homology-based rule assignment goes wrong: elongation factor Tu assigned to sulfate adenylyltransferase in two organisms (CysN is an EF-Tu-like GTPase), CBS-domain proteins to IMP dehydrogenase, a transcriptional regulator to phosphoglycerate mutase, a 3-oxoacyl-ACP synthase to pyruvate kinase, pterin-4α-carbinolamine dehydratase to dihydroxy-acid dehydratase, the next or previous enzyme of a pathway to its neighbour (ArgH to argininosuccinate synthase, HisI to phosphoribosyl-ATP pyrophosphatase, PurM to GAR synthetase, PdxA to PNP synthase). The B. thetaiotaomicron anomaly of the first cycle also resolved from annotation: the genome carries a second, glutamine-dependent carbamoyl-phosphate synthetase (BT3866) that CarveMe never assigned, so the model's rule was missing an isozyme rather than carrying a false one; adding it (rule R4) is the one patch that makes predictions *more* permissive, and it is the one the data confirm both ways — BT0556 and BT0557 become dispensable, as they are, and BT3866 has the phenotype.

Verified against the fitness data on all genes with data (no rich-medium exclusion, so that genes the patches make essential everywhere are not silently dropped from scoring), the two patch sets together change 518 gene–condition predictions in the four draft models: 504 switch to essential, of which 487 (97 percent) are experimentally important, and 14 switch to dispensable, none experimentally important. No wild-type growth is lost. MCC rises in every organism — B. thetaiotaomicron 0.50 → 0.56, S. oneidensis 0.46 → 0.52, P. putida 0.49 → 0.55, S. meliloti 0.52 → 0.62 — with false positives down by 64 to 210 per organism and false negatives essentially unchanged (`patch_v0.1+gpr-v0.2__nodroprich_evaluation.tsv`; the same picture under the Bernstein exclusion convention, `patch_v0.1+gpr-v0.2_vs_v0.1_evaluation.tsv`). The seventeen unsupported switches are single conditions in which a gene that is important everywhere else is not — S. oneidensis biosynthesis genes on casamino acids, where the organism takes up the amino acid and the draft cannot — which is a transporter gap the loop's next iteration should meet, not an error in the rule. One adjudication was expected to be contradicted and was: removing the "dihydroorotase-like" PyrC′ of P. putida from the dihydroorotase rule is right by annotation, but PyrC′ has a structural role in the CPS/ATCase complex and its fitness phenotype is real, so the model now mis-predicts it for a reason no stoichiometric model can represent.

**Third cycle: the rules that matter, and what the gate rejected.** Gene rules can only change a prediction where the reaction is essential in some condition, so the adjudication was then focused on the 257 OR-rule reactions that are essential in at least one growth condition (`scripts/essential_or_rules.py`), of which 135 were changed under the same annotation rules (`gpr_patches_v0.3.json`). Applied on top of the earlier patches, this set switched 391 predictions to essential of which only 21 were experimentally important — the verifier's clearest rejection so far, and an informative one. Attributing the changes to individual patches (each is scored on its own genes) sorts the 28 rejected patches into three classes, all recorded in the file with status "held". First, transporter gaps: riboflavin, thiamine and folate-branch biosynthesis genes became essential in S. oneidensis and S. meliloti whose media (ShewMM, RCH2) contain Wolfe's vitamin mix, which the drafts cannot import. Second, reaction-level essentiality artefacts that a false isozyme had been masking: pyruvate carboxylase in B. thetaiotaomicron (which uses PEP carboxykinase), glycolate oxidase in P. putida, glycolaldehyde dehydrogenase in S. meliloti, and CysQ in B. thetaiotaomicron, whose draft lacks cysteine uptake and so must reduce sulfate although the medium supplies cysteine. Third, adjudications the data show to be wrong: the "ArgC-like" paralog of P. putida is a functional N-acetyl-γ-glutamyl-phosphate reductase, the inositol-monophosphatase-family gene of S. meliloti is not its histidinol phosphatase, and generic aminotransferases should have been kept as alternatives for phenylalanine transamination. The 107 accepted patches are neutral or supported (8 of 8 changed predictions confirmed). The lesson for WS1 is that cleaning gene rules can lower a score by exposing errors elsewhere, so a patch's effect must be read gene by gene, and rejections are the next work list.

**Medium completion.** The first rejected class suggested a fix at the medium rather than the model: every component of a base medium should be importable, and the drafts lack exchanges for the vitamins in Wolfe's mix. Adding an exchange and a gene-less uptake for every medium component the model lacks (`--complete-medium-transport`), with organic components limited to a trace so that hemin, cysteine or vitamins cannot serve as carbon sources, and with pantothenate and folate excluded because their transporters are rare outside the enterobacteria, switched 199 predictions to dispensable: all 126 in S. meliloti and all 28 in P. putida (its RCH2-medium conditions) are confirmed by the fitness data — PABA, NAD-precursor and riboflavin genes without phenotypes — lifting S. meliloti's MCC from 0.62 to 0.66; in S. oneidensis 25 of the 45 changes are wrong, 21 of them from one artefact, a trickle of 5′-deoxyadenosine from adenosylcobalamin uptake that lets purine-biosynthesis knockouts grow at 5 percent of the wild-type rate, above the absolute 1e-3 threshold. A sensitivity check on every result table (`MCC` at the absolute threshold against no-growth defined as below 5 or 10 percent of wild type) changes nothing else by more than 0.003, so the benchmark's conclusions do not depend on the threshold, and a relative criterion would remove this last artefact. Draft-model scores after the accepted patches and medium completion, all genes with fitness data: B. thetaiotaomicron MCC 0.56 (from 0.50 before any patch), P. putida 0.56 (from 0.49), S. oneidensis 0.52 (from 0.46), S. meliloti 0.66 (from 0.52).

The scoring convention matters and is now reported both ways. Bernstein et al. exclude genes the model calls essential with every exchange open, on the grounds that such genes could not be in a transposon library made on rich medium; for draft models that lack transporters this exclusion removes genes whose essentiality the model gets right for the wrong reason (P. putida: 17 genes, whose exclusion lifts the pooled MCC from 0.49 to 0.62) and, after patching, genes whose new essentiality is correct — so the paired evaluations above use all genes, and the headline table keeps the published convention.

## 6. Decisions (continuing the roadmap's log)

D13 (5 Sept 2026): organisms for the multi-organism benchmark are B. thetaiotaomicron VPI-5482, P. putida KT2440, S. oneidensis MR-1 and S. meliloti 1021 (with P. vulgatus, K. grimontii, S. elongatus and N. vulgaris downloaded for later), chosen because each pairs Fitness Browser data with both a draft and a curated model. Rationale: draft-versus-curated on the same yardstick is the comparison that shows what curation is worth, and the gut organisms connect to AGORA2 and the lab's microbiome work.

D14 (5 Sept 2026): draft models are gap-filled for growth on one reference condition only (the experimental base medium plus glucose, or lactate for S. oneidensis), with the added reactions recorded in the card, and both the shipped and the gap-filled variants are scored. Rationale: filling for every tested carbon source would make the condition-level result trivial; filling for one leaves the rest as predictions and keeps the intervention auditable.

D15 (5 Sept 2026): gap-filling forbids any added reaction from running in an oxygen-releasing direction, and transport reactions cost 1.5 times a metabolic reaction. Rationale: §4, second paragraph.

D16 (5 Sept 2026): the media are the Fitness Browser's own recipes mapped to BiGG components, with trace metals (Ni, Se, W, Mo, Zn, Cu) unlimited in every base medium for parity with the Bernstein M9 mapping, and oxygen closed for the anaerobic Varel–Bryant medium. Rationale: draft biomass equations demand trace metals the recipes do not list; making them free everywhere avoids scoring a trace-metal bookkeeping difference as a metabolic prediction.

## 7. Caveats and open items

The compound mapping is the largest source of silent bias: 22 of 47 B. thetaiotaomicron carbon sources have no BiGG identifier or were excluded, most of them oligo- and polysaccharides (arabinogalactan, FOS, heparin, chondroitin sulfate, levan, dextran, cyclodextrin and others), so the organism's glycan-degrading repertoire — its most interesting metabolism — is untested here; a PUL-aware extension is needed. Replicates are averaged per condition and no t-statistic filter is applied, following Bernstein et al.; the Fitness Browser's own significance rule (|t| > 4) would remove noisy calls and should be a reported variant. Genes added by gap-filling carry no gene rule, so their true genes cannot be evaluated. The S. oneidensis fill routes L-lactate through a cytosolic NAD-dependent dehydrogenase rather than the organism's LldEFG complex, so the lactate-condition knockout predictions for that pathway are not meaningful. The DMSO co-solvent in 38 P. putida experiments is treated as inert.

The 63-IEM whole-body run from Sprint 2 completed once but the HiGHS time limit applies to the cumulative run time of a persistent model object, so every IEM after the fourth was skipped as "time limit reached" in zero seconds; the four that ran scored 16 of 19 biomarkers (histidinemia 5/5, AGAT deficiency 2/2, argininemia 6/6, CPS1 deficiency 3/6). The limit is now re-based before every solve and the remaining 59 IEMs are re-running; the Sprint 2 note will be updated when they finish.

## 8. Next

In order: scale the gene-rule adjudication of §5 — the four draft models carry 2,145 OR-rules, a keyword screen written this sprint (`scripts/check_gene_rules.py`) flags 1,097 of them and recovers 36 of the 51 hand-adjudicated changes, so the next pass is to adjudicate every OR-rule with its annotations in batches under rules R1–R4 and let the benchmark score the lot, which is the first curation task large enough to show what a frontier model adds over a heuristic; then the reaction-level bypasses (reversible amino-acid reactions such as serine acetyltransferase and serine–pyruvate transaminase run backwards, transporter gaps exposed by the casamino-acid conditions), each correction verified across the four organisms and the E. coli control with the acceptance rate reported honestly; the remaining curated-model arms of the same table (iJN1463 is done; iSO783, iGD1575 and iAH991 are in supplementary material or VMH, and AGORA2 needs Tim's VMH download), which turns the benchmark into the draft-versus-curated comparison the paper needs; the B. thetaiotaomicron glycan conditions through a polysaccharide-aware mapping; and the whole-body IEM results once the run completes, followed by the physiological-constraint port.
