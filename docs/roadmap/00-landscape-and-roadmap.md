## 0. How to use this document

This is the standing charter for the project. It records where constraint-based metabolic modelling stands in September 2026, why it under-delivers relative to its promise, what frontier-model intelligence can and cannot change about that, and a staged programme of work with success criteria. Every future session in this project should read this document first, then the decision log in section 8, then whichever evidence brief is relevant.

The evidence behind the claims here lives in six companion briefs in this project (`evidence/01` to `evidence/06`), each compiled from primary sources with URLs. Where a figure could not be verified in-session it is marked as such in the brief and is not relied on here. Nothing in this document should be quoted in a manuscript without re-checking the source, because several of the 2026 items are preprints.

The document is versioned. Sections 8 and 9 (decisions and current sprint) are expected to change every few weeks; sections 1 to 7 should change only when the diagnosis changes.

## 1. Executive summary

Constraint-based modelling has become large without becoming reliable. The community now holds hundreds of thousands of genome-scale reconstructions, whole-body models with more than eighty thousand reactions, and infant models drawn from more than two million newborn-screening profiles. Yet three facts from the last three years define the field's actual condition. First, the same genomes fed to three standard reconstruction tools yield reaction sets with a Jaccard similarity of only 0.23 to 0.24 even after identifier harmonisation (roughly a third of each model's reactions in common), and downstream community predictions depend more on which tool was used than on the biology. Second, in the APOLLO pipeline, automated draft models agreed with experiment about ten percent of the time and rose to seventy to eighty percent only after refinement against a curated knowledge base that had itself taken a manual review of 732 papers to build; that knowledge base is the field's most valuable asset, and extending it to new taxa, phenotypes and reactions is its least scalable activity. Third, there is no shared, held-out, machine-readable benchmark for what a metabolic model is supposed to predict; quality is scored by structural checks such as MEMOTE, which measure structure rather than prediction and can diverge from it, and the research found no community challenge that has ever been run.

The software situation compounds this. The MATLAB COBRA Toolbox holds many of the field's most powerful capabilities (whole-body models, DEMETER curation, most of Persephone, thermodynamic and annotation tooling) behind a commercial language with no published migration path. COBRApy, the de facto Python core, is maintained by a very small group, has had every release since 0.26 cut by a single maintainer, carries a multi-year pull-request backlog, and supports SBML FBC version 2 but not version 3. Several packages in the Python ecosystem around it have had no release since 2020 to 2022. Reproducibility across tools is guaranteed by nothing: only about forty percent of flux-balance models submitted to BioModels reproduced without intervention under the FROG protocol (all did with help), and quantities that users compare informally across tools, such as the number of active reactions, are not even well-defined under degenerate optima.

On the clinical side, whole-body models predict the direction of change of known inborn-error biomarkers about eighty-five percent of the time, which is a real achievement, but the field has not moved from direction to magnitude, no model-based tool has prospective clinical evaluation, and the best-known biomarker-prediction method drops to ten percent recall when confronted with a real clinical database rather than a curated test set.

Frontier models are strongest exactly where the field's bottleneck sits: integrating biochemistry, genomics, physiology, optimisation and software knowledge that very few individuals hold at once. In this project "frontier intelligence" means, first, a frontier model doing that integrative work directly — curating, benchmarking, deriving, porting, writing — under the same verification discipline as any collaborator; building language-model agents into the field's tools is a secondary aim, pursued where it has a clear place. But the 2026 evidence is consistent that ungrounded language-model output cannot be trusted for this work. Models asked to write metabolic networks from memory mostly produce ones that cannot carry flux; asked to find injected stoichiometric errors by conversation, they fail, and succeed only when made to call the tools; asked to name enzyme commission numbers from memory, they perform at or below sequence-neighbour voting. The successes reported so far share a pattern: proposals are checked against something external, whether manual review, a held-out test set, executable tools or a wet-lab result, before they count, and provenance is kept.

The strategy that follows from this is to build the verifiers first and then run frontier intelligence through them at scale. Concretely, the programme has a spine and five workstreams. The spine (WS0) is a benchmark and conformance infrastructure that turns "is this model better?" into a measurable question. Around it sit verifier-gated curation at scale (WS1), methods that report uncertainty rather than point estimates (WS2), a Python software spine that carries the Toolbox's unique capabilities forward and is designed to be driven by agents as well as people (WS3), a rare-disease and clinical proving ground that ties directly to the lab's inborn-error work (WS4), and access and education (WS5) so that the field stops requiring a rare skill stack to enter. The first ninety days build the yardstick and demonstrate the propose-then-verify loop on public models; the first eighteen months aim at a public benchmark in external use, a hundred verifier-gated curation changes merged into community models, the lab's whole-body capabilities running in Python, and an inborn-error benchmark with a first calibrated-magnitude pilot.

## 2. Where the field stands in 2026

### 2.1 Models and curation

The volume of reconstructions has outrun any ability to validate them. AGORA2 delivered 7,302 curated microbial strains in 2023 with uptake and secretion accuracy of 0.81 to 0.84 against experiment, at the cost of a manual literature review of 732 papers; APOLLO scaled the same pipeline to 247,092 metagenome-assembled reconstructions in 2025, where KBase drafts agreed with experiment only ten to eleven percent of the time and seventy-three to eighty-one percent after automated DEMETER refinement, which reuses the knowledge encoded during AGORA2's curation. The lesson is that a curated knowledge base, not per-model reading, is what scales; the bottleneck is extending that knowledge base to taxa, phenotypes and reactions it does not yet cover. On the human side, Human-GEM 2.0 (the Human2 paper, PNAS, April 2026) is the first community reconstruction to use a language model systematically in curation (Human-GEM had used GPT-3 once in 2021 to prune non-metabolic genes): a GPT-4 audit of all 26,246 gene–reaction pairs classified 7,774 as consistent and 2,195 as inconsistent, but left 16,277, or sixty-two percent, inconclusive, and the resolved ones still went through manual review before 1,135 gene–protein–reaction rules were changed. The authors attribute the inconclusive majority to incomplete annotation of the genes themselves, so that number is a ceiling set jointly by model capability and by unknown gene function; how much of it retrieval and reasoning can recover is an open and measurable question.

Tool disagreement is now quantified. Hsieh and colleagues showed in 2024 that CarveMe, gapseq and KBase applied to the same 105 metagenome-assembled genomes produce reaction sets with a Jaccard similarity of only 0.23 to 0.24 after MetaNetX harmonisation (about a third of each model's reactions in common), and that the metabolites predicted to be exchanged in a community are determined more by the tool than by the community. MACAW, a 2025 suite of structural tests, flags forty percent of Human-GEM 1.15 reactions, forty-eight percent of yeast-GEM 9.0 and fifty-two percent of iML1515 as potentially problematic, while its authors stress that a flag is not an error and resolution stays manual. Energy-generating cycles keep surfacing after publication: AGORA2 needed a post-release patch to 151 models. Thermodynamic curation is blocked upstream of the model by chemistry: sixty-one percent of species in the 2026 EHMN human network lack formulas, and a fifth of BiGG models cannot be converted to a thermodynamic form automatically; the Toolbox's MetaboAnnotator (2022) exists for exactly this formula, charge and structure assignment problem and is part of what needs carrying forward rather than rebuilding. Identifier namespaces remain fragmented across BiGG (legacy site frozen since 2019, relaunched as bigg.bio), MetaNetX (which concedes contradictory cross-references), ModelSEED, KEGG (licence-restricted for bulk use) and VMH. MEMOTE, the field's quality standard, has had no release since January 2024. Full details and sources are in evidence brief 01.

### 2.2 Methods

The methodological frontier has moved, but unevenly. Flux sampling can now be done on Recon3D-scale models in under a day (dingo, 2024) and in the loopless space orders of magnitude faster than before (LooplessFluxSampler, 2024); COBRApy 0.32 finally ships a uniform sampler. Bayesian and ensemble approaches exist (BayFlux for 13C-constrained posteriors; GEMsembler, which lifts E. coli essentiality AUC-PR from 0.56 to 0.71 by ensembling reconstruction tools) but are not defaults. The most important 2025 finding for omics integration is that the choice of expression threshold and normalisation changes results more than the choice of extraction algorithm, and a 2026 review states plainly that head-to-head comparisons remain sparse. Enzyme-constrained modelling is mature in MATLAB (GECKO 3) but rests on predicted kinetics: experimental turnover numbers cover under thirty percent of iML1515's enzyme–reaction pairs, and the best predictor (CatPred) reaches R² of 0.61 in-distribution and 0.39 out of distribution. Whole-body and ME-models expose a numerical frontier that the Python ecosystem does not address: whole-body models need careful scaling and solver-specific settings in double precision (the Toolbox's optimizeWBModel exists for that reason), ME-models span six orders of magnitude and need quad-precision solves, and both currently depend on CPLEX, MOSEK or specialised solvers. Machine-learning hybrids are promising where data are dense (the differentiable AMN family, FlowGAT for essentiality) but have been validated almost entirely on E. coli. Sources are in evidence brief 02.

### 2.3 Tooling and infrastructure

COBRApy released 0.32.1 on 11 August 2026; every release since 0.26 has been cut by a single maintainer, twenty pull requests are open including a positively reviewed contribution adding FBC-v3 metadata support that has waited since April 2025, and the release cadence has fallen from twenty-two releases in 2017 to one to three per year. It reads and writes FBC version 2, which is what BiGG, BioModels and Human-GEM ship, but not version 3, which was finalised around 2023 and adds user-defined constraints, key–value metadata and quadratic objectives. The COBRA Toolbox is at v3.7 with zero open pull requests, a published plan toward v4.0, and no published Python path; its capabilities with no Python equivalent include the whole-body and PSCM toolbox, DEMETER, rBioNet, XomicsToModel, thermoKernel, MetaboAnnotator and most of Persephone (whose MARS component is already Python). The active Python ecosystem is real but narrow: straindesign, MICOM, CNApy, coralME and COMETSpy released in 2026, while cameo, pytfa, dfba, geckopy and MASSpy have had no release since 2020 to 2022, and MEMOTE and Escher have slowed. Solvers are a licensing and numerics problem rather than a speed problem: Gurobi is fastest and HiGHS is competitive on LP, but academic Gurobi web licences now require a permanent connection and cap concurrent sessions, GPU first-order LP methods have never been benchmarked on GEMs and cannot return basic solutions, and in the one published solver benchmark GLPK and COIN could not finish the Recon3D MILP within a week while SCIP and HiGHS could. Reproducibility is a definitional problem as much as a numerical one. Users compare quantities such as the number of active reactions across tools and find three-fold differences, but under non-unique optima such counts are not reproducible in principle, which is why the FROG protocol scores objective values, flux variability and deletions instead; loopless methods have been non-deterministic in past releases; about forty percent of BioModels flux-balance submissions reproduced unaided under FROG, though all could be reproduced with intervention, and while some reference FROG tooling is unmaintained, FBCModelTests.jl is an actively maintained implementation. Agentic interfaces exist but are barely evaluated: a handful of MCP servers, one general bio-agent with a single FBA tool, and three 2026 preprints (ChatGEM, MechAInistic, the NUS evaluation), scored by language-model judges or single case studies rather than ground truth, with ChatGEM additionally reporting one wet-lab validation. Sources are in evidence brief 03.

### 2.4 Clinical and rare-disease use

This is the lab's flagship territory and the strongest evidence in the field. Harvey and Harvetta predict the direction of 252 known biofluid biomarkers across 57 inborn errors at 85.3 and 84.9 percent, against 50.2 percent for Recon3D; infant whole-body models track WHO growth curves and reproduce IEMbase biomarker directions; virtual knockouts predict the direction of population gene–metabolite associations at 73 to 77 percent in 4,737 exome-plus-metabolomics participants. The lab's Recon4IMD consortium (€7.87M, to May 2027) holds roughly 1,945 diagnosed and 685 undiagnosed patients and explicitly targets regulatory-grade decision support, and the ERC proof-of-concept award for iChatRD (building on the ChatRD prototype) in June 2026 is the first funded effort the research found to couple metabolic models with language models for diagnosis. Against that, the limits are equally clear. Inborn-error biomarker predictions are direction-only (growth and basal metabolic rate are quantitative); the one attempt at biomarker magnitude (dynamic infant PBPK–WBM coupling) underestimates patient concentrations. The infant whole-body models cover 595 of 1,364 inborn-error genes. The 2009 Shlomi benchmark remains the cautionary tale: precision 0.73 on a curated set, precision 0.41 and recall 0.10 on a clinical database, with missing transporters, absent regulation and network incompleteness as the named causes, and those failure modes have not been systematically closed since. No model-based tool has prospective clinical evaluation, and the language-model side is no better: a March 2026 meta-analysis of LLM rare-disease diagnosis found pooled top-1 recall of 43 percent with every study at high risk of leakage. Sources are in evidence brief 04.

### 2.5 Validation data and benchmarks

The ground truth exists but is scattered and partly frozen. The Fitness Browser holds 7,593 genome-wide RB-TnSeq experiments across fifty bacteria with per-organism downloads; BacDive holds phenotypes for more than a hundred thousand strains under CC-BY; Keio, DEG and DepMap cover essentiality; BRENDA and eQuilibrator cover kinetics and thermodynamics. But the only fluxomics compendium (CeCaFDB) was last updated in 2015, growth-phenotype panels live in supplementary tables, media recipes are machine-readable in MediaDive and KOMODO but there is no maintained conversion from recipe to exchange bounds (the hardest part of any phenotype benchmark), and inborn-error biomarker knowledge is split between IEMbase, which has no documented bulk access, and the biomarker tables in the lab's own 2012 compendium and 2020 whole-body paper, which are the natural seed for a harmonised ground truth but are not yet linked to OMIM, Orphanet, HPO or HMDB. The best current validation practice, Bernstein and colleagues' 2023 evaluation of four E. coli model generations against fitness data over 25 carbon sources, showed that naive accuracy metrics can make models look worse as they improve and that AUC-PR with explicit media is the honest measure; it also showed that most false predictions fall into three fixable classes: medium cofactors, isoenzyme rules and reversibility. The research found no DREAM-style challenge for metabolic models and no 2024–2026 standard benchmark, and the field's automated output (6,239 models by 2019, only 183 manually reconstructed; 247,092 in one 2025 release) vastly outpaces its validation. Sources are in evidence brief 05.

### 2.6 What language models and agents have done so far

The 2026 record is instructive. On the positive side: Human2 used GPT-4 to triage every gene–reaction pair in Human-GEM and shipped a continuous-integration stack (metabolic tasks, MEMOTE, MACAW) that blocks bad merges; D2Cell mined 29,006 metabolic-engineering results from the literature at 85 percent precision; ChatGEM's retrieval-augmented agents produced a strain-design prediction that was validated experimentally; MetaKnogic reaches 0.98 mechanistic accuracy when it checks its claims against a curated reaction network. On the negative side, and more important for design: frontier models asked to generate metabolic networks de novo recover 64 to 91 percent of core E. coli reactions but most of the networks cannot carry flux (Tewari et al., eLife, July 2026); conversational prompting fails to find injected stoichiometric errors that tool-grounded prompting finds (Yeoh et al., June 2026); direct language-model generation of SBML was one hundred percent invalid in the one study that tried it; closed-book EC-number assignment by LLMs is uniformly poor. Enzyme and transporter foundation models (HIT-EC, CLEAN, SPOT, CatPred) are strong enough to be useful as evidence sources but none has been integrated into a reconstruction pipeline. No benchmark exists for any of these tasks. Sources are in evidence brief 06.

## 3. Diagnosis: six bottlenecks, and what intelligence changes

### 3.1 The field cannot measure improvement

This is the root bottleneck and everything else is downstream of it. Without a held-out, leakage-controlled, machine-readable benchmark, a "better" model is one with a higher MEMOTE score or a larger reaction count, neither of which measures prediction (Bernstein's E. coli series shows accuracy did rise across model generations, but only a phenotype benchmark could show it, and naive metrics suggested the opposite). Method papers are incomparable, reconstruction tools disagree without consequence, and curated models risk being validated on the same data that shaped them. A frontier model can build this yardstick almost entirely from public data: the ground truth exists, the metrics are known (AUC-PR with explicit media, per Bernstein 2023), and the engineering is the kind of careful, tedious, cross-format work that intelligence does well. This is intelligence-limited, not data-limited, and it is the highest-leverage thing to build.

### 3.2 Curation is a knowledge-integration bottleneck

What the AGORA2 literature review that built DEMETER's knowledge base and Human2's manual audit have in common is that each decision requires holding biochemistry (is this reaction real, balanced, directional, in this compartment?), genomics (is this gene an enzyme, which isoform, which complex?), physiology (what is the medium, what is the biomass?) and optimisation intuition (why does this loop or blocked reaction arise?) in one head. That is precisely the cross-disciplinary stack that makes the field niche, and precisely what a frontier model with retrieval can do at scale. The evidence says it works only as propose-then-verify: the model drafts a change with citations, checkers (mass and charge balance, structural tests such as MACAW and MEMOTE, energy-cycle detection, thermodynamic plausibility as evidence rather than as a hard rule, and, crucially, regression against the benchmark of 3.1) accept or reject it, and the change carries provenance. Verifier gating is not new to the field: DEMETER's tests, ModelSEED 2's ATP-yield check and Human-GEM's continuous-integration stack all do it for their own pipelines; what is new is making the proposer a frontier model and the gate general and measured. Human2's sixty-two percent inconclusive is the number to move; Yeoh's finding that tools succeed where chat fails is the design constraint.

### 3.3 The software spine is thin and split

Most of the lab's most powerful capabilities are MATLAB-only; the Python core is thinly maintained and behind on standards; reproducibility across tools is not guaranteed. This is what makes methods non-composable and keeps non-specialists out. Frontier models change this in two ways. Porting with differential testing against the MATLAB implementation as oracle is a task where model-driven engineering is now reliable, provided a conformance suite defines correctness. And maintenance (triage, rebasing, standards support) is exactly the low-glamour, high-value work a model can multiply. This is the workstream where Tim's existing translation project already sits, and the roadmap's job is to give it a definition of done.

### 3.4 The statistical culture is point estimates

Degenerate linear programmes are reported as single flux vectors; biomarkers as arrows; method choice as a coin flip; kinetic parameters as point predictions with R² of 0.4 out of distribution. Clinical use needs calibrated intervals on magnitudes. The mathematics (sampling, ensembles, Bayesian treatment of alternative optima, robust optimisation with parameter uncertainty) is known, and a frontier model can derive, implement and verify formulations; but adoption depends on the yardstick, because uncertainty is only worth reporting when it is scored.

### 3.5 The data the models need is literature-locked or missing

Formulas and charges for orphan metabolites, transporter substrates and tissue localisation, organism-specific biomass and media, phenotype tables, fluxomics, inborn-error biomarker magnitudes by biofluid and age: much of this exists in papers and supplementary tables and not in any database. Extraction with provenance at ninety-plus percent precision is a demonstrated language-model capability (D2Cell, BioPromptX, MetaKnogic). What intelligence cannot do is replace measurement: kinetics out of distribution, regulation, absolute concentrations and physiological parameters are measurement-limited, and the honest contribution there is to prioritise which experiments would most reduce model uncertainty.

### 3.6 The field is small and its skill stack is rare

Industry adoption is described as in its infancy, systems-biology programmes struggle to teach biology, programming and mathematics together, and the tools require all three. Making the field measurable (3.1), composable (3.3) and credible in the clinic (WS4) addresses the cause. Language-model interfaces address the symptom, and carry a specific risk that MechAInistic names: plausible-sounding but unverifiable narratives. Access therefore must be built on the same verifier gates as curation, or it will make the field less trustworthy rather than more.

## 4. Operating principles

These are the rules every workstream follows. They are written so that a future session can check any piece of work against them.

Nothing enters a model without a deterministic check and a provenance record. A language model may propose a reaction, rule, bound, annotation or fix; only a checker (mass and charge balance, structural tests, benchmark regression, with thermodynamic estimates treated as evidence rather than as a hard rule because standard-state free energies alone do not decide feasibility) may accept it, and the accepted change records what evidence supported it and which checks it passed. Where no deterministic checker exists, as for literature extraction or a diagnostic agent, the equivalent rule is expert-audited precision on a held-out sample and leakage-controlled evaluation, and the output is labelled as evidence rather than as a model change.

Every method or model claim is scored on held-out data with leakage documented. Benchmark cards state which ground truth touched curation. In-sample numbers are reported beside held-out numbers, never instead of them.

Prefer improving the yardstick over improving the score. When a benchmark turns out to be wrong (a mis-specified medium, a mislabelled phenotype), fixing the benchmark comes before fixing the model.

Uncertainty is a first-class output. Predictions carry intervals or probabilities where the mathematics allows it, and those intervals are scored for calibration.

Build for two users at once: the person and the agent. Every capability exposes typed inputs and outputs, provenance, and a test, so that it can be called by a person in a notebook or by an agent in a loop with equal safety.

Open and licence-clean by default. Public models, CC-BY data, MIT or GPL code; KEGG bulk data and other restricted sources are not depended on; HMDB's non-commercial licence is respected in anything that could become a product.

Do not fragment the community. New code interoperates with COBRApy through SBML Level 3 FBC and a compatibility adapter; fixes that belong upstream go upstream.

Verified before cited. Project documents tag claims as verified or unverified; nothing unverified enters a manuscript, grant text or talk.

## 5. The programme

### WS0 — The yardstick: benchmark and conformance infrastructure

WS0 is the spine. Its aim is that "is this model, method or tool better?" becomes a question with a numerical, reproducible answer. It has three parts.

The first is GEM-Bench, a harness that takes any SBML model and scores it against public ground truth with explicit media definitions: gene essentiality and conditional fitness (Keio, Fitness Browser, DEG, DepMap for human lines), growth and substrate phenotypes (BacDive, ProTraits, per-study Biolog panels), uptake and secretion rates (NCI-60 and AGORA2 validation sets), and fluxes against 13C-MFA where available. The hardest and least glamorous component is media: a maintained conversion from MediaDive and KOMODO recipes to exchange bounds with stated assumptions, because most apparent prediction failures are medium mis-specifications. Metrics are AUC-PR and MCC with confidence intervals, following Bernstein 2023, plus a leakage card per model stating which ground truth was used during its curation and, separately, which ground truth a frontier model has almost certainly seen in training (Keio, the Fitness Browser and iML1515 all predate model cut-offs), so that held-out organisms and newly deposited strains carry the weight of any claim about model-driven curation. The initial organism set is E. coli iML1515 (for which Bernstein's dataset already serves as a regression test), Yeast9, three to five AGORA2 strains with BacDive phenotypes, and Human-GEM against DepMap, chosen because each has both a curated model and public phenotypes. GEM-Bench should be co-owned from the start with the groups who hold the data and the prior benchmarks (the Fitness Browser and Bernstein groups, gapseq, Human-GEM, openCOBRA), because a benchmark perceived as one lab's instrument will not be adopted. Success at twelve months is a public benchmark covering at least twenty organisms, reproduced by at least one independent group within 0.02 AUC-PR, with two external groups using it; fifty organisms and three external groups is the eighteen-month stretch target.

The second is a cross-tool conformance suite in the spirit of FROG: objective value, flux variability, gene and reaction deletions, computed for a fixed set of BiGG and BioModels models across COBRApy, the COBRA Toolbox, COBREXA and cobrar, and across GLPK, HiGHS, SCIP, Gurobi and CPLEX, run in continuous integration. Its immediate product is a divergence matrix; its lasting product is root-caused fixes upstream (tolerances, default bounds, loop-removal semantics). Success is a published divergence matrix for at least one hundred models and at least five root-caused fixes accepted upstream. This suite is also what gives WS3's Python work a definition of correctness.

The third is a set of grounded tasks for agents and language models: reaction balancing, cross-namespace identifier mapping, gene–protein–reaction inference against Yeast9 and iML1515, held-out-reaction gap-filling, MACAW and MEMOTE defect repair, and standard COBRApy analyses, each with numeric ground truth so that agent evaluations stop relying on language-model judges. This replaces the rubric scoring used by every 2026 agent paper found. A stretch goal for WS0, which the lab is well placed to lead, is a recurring community challenge in the CASP mould, predicting phenotypes for newly deposited strains or newly screened organisms. The research found no such challenge has been run; a frontier model can do most of the administrative and engineering work of running one, and newly generated phenotypes are also the cleanest answer to training-data leakage.

### WS1 — Verifier-gated curation at scale

WS1 generalises the pattern that DEMETER, ModelSEED 2, Human-GEM's continuous integration, gapsmith-db and MetaKnogic have each built for their own pipelines into a general, measured capability with a frontier model as proposer. In this project the proposer is the frontier model working directly in project sessions (packaging the loop as an agent for other groups is a later, optional step): it reads a model, its flags (MACAW, MEMOTE, energy-generating cycles, blocked reactions, inconclusive gene–reaction pairs) and the literature, proposes specific changes with citations, and submits them to a gate that checks mass and charge balance, structural tests, energy-cycle absence and regression against GEM-Bench, with eQuilibrator free-energy estimates attached as evidence for directionality decisions rather than used as a veto (they are unavailable for exactly the orphan metabolites that need curation, and standard-state values do not decide feasibility). Accepted changes become pull requests with provenance.

The first targets, in order, are the 16,277 inconclusive gene–reaction pairs in Human-GEM 2.0 (the measurable goal is to reduce the inconclusive fraction as far as the evidence allows at precision matching the manual reviewers on a held-out sample, and to report how much of the remainder is capped by genuinely unknown gene function), formula and charge assignment for orphan metabolites building on the Toolbox's MetaboAnnotator so that thermodynamic curation becomes possible, cross-namespace reaction reconciliation using chemical structure rather than string identifiers (so that the 0.23 Jaccard between tools can be decomposed into representation differences and real disagreements), and flag-to-fix proposals for MACAW and energy-cycle output on iML1515, Yeast9 and a subset of AGORA2. In parallel, literature-to-evidence extraction rebuilds the DEMETER phenotype tables automatically for species outside AGORA2 and grows machine-readable growth-phenotype and fermentation-product tables, at a target of ninety-five percent precision on expert audit. Success at eighteen months is at least one hundred maintainer-merged, verifier-gated changes across community models, with no net degradation on GEM-Bench held-out sets and a reduction in post-release patches of the AGORA2 v2.01 kind.

### WS2 — Methods that report uncertainty

WS2 makes uncertainty the default output and makes method choice a scored decision. Its components are: certified flux sampling at genome scale in Python (porting the fastest 2024 samplers with convergence diagnostics and a loopless option, targeting Recon3D loopless samples in under a day); ensemble and Bayesian prediction over alternative optima and alternative reconstructions, scored for calibration against fitness data (target expected calibration error below 0.05); uncertainty-aware enzyme-constrained models that propagate CatPred-style predictive variances instead of point turnover numbers; a living benchmark of omics-integration methods across thresholds and normalisations on Human-GEM and Recon3D with 13C and CRISPR ground truth, from which a learned method selector can be trained; and the numerical work needed for whole-body and ME-scale models in Python (scaling diagnostics, double-to-quad refinement, solution certification, open-solver fallbacks), with the target of solving Harvey and Harvetta to a residual of 1e-9 without CPLEX at no more than twice the current runtime. Formal derivation and verification of community-model formulations (MICOM, SteadyCom, cFBA equivalences and degeneracies) is a smaller, mathematics-heavy component well suited to a frontier model. All of WS2 depends on WS0 for scoring and on WS3 for a home.

### WS3 — The Python spine

WS3 was written around Tim's COBRA Toolbox-to-Python translation project; as of September 2026 that project has picked up no steam and is not commencing (D10), so the Python spine is instead built directly in this project as a set of COBRApy-based extensions, with the frontier model doing the porting and the MATLAB Toolbox as oracle whenever Tim can provide MATLAB access. Correctness is still defined by the WS0 conformance suite: a capability is done when it agrees with the MATLAB implementation within tolerance on a fixed fixture set, and Sprint 1 delivered the Python side of that harness (FROG-style fixtures and comparator). The build order follows what WS2 and WS4 need first: loading whole-body models with their coupling constraints and solving them with open solvers (the Toolbox's test-model repository ships Harvey and Harvetta 1.03d, so this can start immediately), then the IEM biomarker protocol on those models, then DEMETER-style refinement, then the remaining MATLAB parts of Persephone (MARS is already Python), thermoKernel, MetaboAnnotator and XomicsToModel. Every module is designed for two users: typed, documented, testable functions callable from a notebook or from an agent, with provenance on outputs.

Standards work sits here too: SBML Level 3 FBC version 3 read and write (finalised around 2023 and still absent from the Python core), a cobra-compatible adapter so that MICOM, straindesign and the rest of the living ecosystem keep working regardless of the core, and a solver-configuration advisor for large models. A maintainer-multiplier effort toward COBRApy (triaging the backlog, rebasing the FBC-v3 and gap-filling pull requests) is cheap, builds goodwill, and reduces fragmentation risk; the decision on how far to go with it is logged in section 8. Finally, an audited natural-language interface (an MCP server with an architect-and-reviewer pattern) is built on top of the same typed API and scored on the WS0 agent tasks; it is not released without that score. Success at eighteen months is at least three Toolbox-only capabilities running in Python and passing conformance, FBC v3 round-tripping, and the agent interface scoring at least ninety percent on the numeric WS0 tasks, with expert grading reserved for the interpretive parts of one hundred standard analyses.

### WS4 — Rare disease and the clinic

WS4 is the proving ground and the bridge to Recon4IMD and ChatRD. Its first deliverable is a harmonised ground truth: the lab already holds the seed in the 2012 inborn-error compendium and the 57-disease, 252-biomarker table behind the 2020 whole-body paper, and the work is to turn these into a versioned, licence-clean benchmark of inborn-error-to-biomarker tuples (disease, gene, metabolite, biofluid, direction, magnitude where known, age and sex strata) linked to OMIM, Orphadata, HPO and HMDB reference ranges and extended by literature extraction with provenance, aiming at a thousand or more triples, reproducing the published 85 percent whole-body figures, and reporting per-disease precision and recall for the first time. The second is the move from direction to magnitude: calibrated prediction intervals for twenty or more inborn errors against newborn-screening and diagnostic ranges, reporting coverage and false-positive rate at screening cut-offs. The third is coverage: verifier-gated curation (WS1) of the roughly forty percent of inborn-error genes absent from the infant whole-body models, and a systematic attack on the transporter, regulation and incompleteness failure modes that Shlomi named in 2009, with the explicit target of raising clinical-database recall above the 0.10 baseline on a held-out patient set. The fourth is the hybrid: whole-body-model-derived candidate metabolites as priors for a language-model diagnostic agent, evaluated on a leakage-controlled cohort; the natural test set is the Recon4IMD patients who receive a diagnosis after enrolment, since undiagnosed patients carry no label, and any use of that cohort is entirely under consortium governance. The target is a ten-point gain in top-1 recall over the language model alone. Because Tim already works on ChatRD's CE marking, WS4 also produces the artefact that regulation will demand: a reproducible, version-pinned validation dossier for one decision-support claim, with dataset provenance, per-disease performance, failure modes and drift monitoring, scoped to both the EU Medical Device Regulation and the EU AI Act, under which a language-model-based decision-support system is a high-risk AI system with obligations phasing in from 2027. Everything in WS4 that touches patient data waits for the lab's governance; everything else starts on public data now.

### WS5 — Access and education

WS5 addresses the field's niche-ness directly and is deliberately last in dependency order, because access built on unverified tooling would harm the field. It consists of, if and when the lab wants it, the audited natural-language interface from WS3 released with its WS0 score; agent-readable documentation and typed tool descriptions for the whole Python spine; a set of teaching notebooks that walk from a genome to a scored model on GEM-Bench; and, as the benchmark matures, the community challenge described under WS0. Success is measured in adoption (external users of the benchmark and interface) and in reduced time-to-first-scored-model for a newcomer, which can be measured with students.

## 7. Honest limits and risks

Some things this programme cannot do, and should not claim to. Intelligence does not replace measurement: turnover numbers out of distribution, regulation, absolute metabolite concentrations and physiological parameters are limited by data that does not exist, and the contribution there is to extract what is already published and to rank which experiments would most reduce model uncertainty. The clinical workstream cannot produce prospective validation on its own; it can produce the benchmark, the calibrated predictions and the dossier that make a prospective study designable. And no amount of engineering makes a degenerate linear programme non-degenerate; what changes is that the degeneracy is reported.

The specific risks, and what is done about each, are these. Language-model hallucination of identifiers, stoichiometry and citations is the central technical risk; it is mitigated by the gate in every workstream, by retrieval over structured databases rather than parametric memory (the EC-Reason-Bench lesson), and by red-teaming with injected errors. Training-data leakage into benchmarks is the central methodological risk, since frontier models have read BiGG, Keio, the Fitness Browser and the curated models themselves; it is mitigated with post-cutoff curations, newly deposited strains and newly screened organisms, held-out organisms for any claim about model-driven curation, and leakage cards that say so explicitly. Community fragmentation is the central social risk: a third core library, or a benchmark perceived as one lab's instrument, would set the field back; the mitigations are the compatibility adapter, upstream contributions, and inviting the openCOBRA maintainers and the Human-GEM, gapseq and MICOM groups into the benchmark early. Solver licensing and numerics are a practical risk for the whole-body work; mitigation is the open-solver path in WS2 and explicit reliance on the lab's existing CPLEX/MOSEK access for the oracle runs. Regulatory scope creep is a risk for WS4; the dossier is scoped to one claim. Finally, the risk of over-reach: this roadmap covers all four areas because Tim asked it to, but the first ninety days are deliberately narrow.

## 8. Decision log

Decisions taken so far under the "run with it" mandate are recorded here with their rationale so that they can be reversed deliberately rather than drifted from.

D1 (4 Sept 2026): the benchmark comes before the curation agent. Rationale: without a held-out yardstick, curation cannot be shown to help and could be overfitting; the benchmark also enables every other workstream.

D2 (4 Sept 2026): propose-then-verify is the only permitted pattern for language-model changes to models; where no deterministic verifier exists (literature extraction, diagnostic agents), the output is labelled evidence rather than model change and is held to expert-audited precision and leakage-controlled evaluation. Rationale: the 2026 evaluations (Tewari, Yeoh, KinModGPT, EC-Reason-Bench) show ungrounded generation is unreliable and tool-grounded loops work.

D3 (4 Sept 2026): Python is the spine language, SBML Level 3 FBC v3 the interchange format, and a cobra-compatible adapter maintains ecosystem interoperability whatever the core. Rationale: the living ecosystem (MICOM, straindesign, coralME) depends on cobra; fragmentation would cost more than it gains. This decision leaves open whether the translation project becomes a new core or a set of extensions; see open question Q1.

D4 (4 Sept 2026): licence-clean data only; no dependency on KEGG bulk data; HMDB used with its non-commercial terms in mind. Rationale: anything that reaches a product or a regulated device must be redistributable.

D5 (4 Sept 2026): public models and data first; lab assets when Tim brings them in. Rationale: Tim's instruction for this phase.

D6 (4 Sept 2026): every project document distinguishes verified from unverified claims, and unverified claims do not enter manuscripts. Rationale: several key 2026 results are preprints and some figures could not be confirmed in-session.

D7 (4 Sept 2026, after independent review of this draft): Phase 0 leads with the three cheap and uncontested pieces (the E. coli regression test, the IEM tuple harmonisation, the differential-test harness) rather than with a full benchmark build, and GEM-Bench is planned as a co-owned community resource with scaled-down twelve-month targets. ## 9. Current sprint

Sprint 3 (5 September 2026) is complete and its results are in the Sprint 3 note: GEM-Bench now runs the carbon-source fitness protocol for any organism with Fitness Browser data, with curated media and compound mappings and complete gene-identifier maps; four EMBL draft models (B. thetaiotaomicron, P. putida, S. oneidensis, S. meliloti) were scored against 25–43 carbon-source conditions each.
